# What the register shows
GEO is the circular orbit about 35,786 km above the equator, where a satellite stays over one spot [register: config.geoAltitudeKm]. Perigee is the lowest point of an orbit. Eccentricity is how far an orbit is from a perfect circle. 0 is a circle [docs/methodology.md: definition of eccentricity].
- Name and NORAD id: MEASAT-3, 29648 [register: norad].
- Launch date: 2006-12-11 [register: launchDate].
- The catalogue lists it as a retired payload. Status source: SATCAT [register: status] [register: statusSource].
- Years since launch at the snapshot date: 19. This is a count since launch, not an age at the end of operation [register: verdict.yearsSinceLaunch].
- Perigee above GEO: +121.4 km [register: pKm].
- Apogee above GEO: +177.8 km [register: qKm].
- Eccentricity: 0.0006665 [register: e].
- Verdict: unquiet, not borderline [register: verdict.verdict] [register: verdict.borderline]. Unquiet means the orbit reaches the protected zone, which extends 200 km either side of GEO [register: config.protectedZoneKm].

# What the sources say
- The IADC guidelines are voluntary: organisations are "encouraged" to use them [IADC-02-01 Revision 2: https://orbitaldebris.jsc.nasa.gov/library/IADC-Space-Debris-Guidelines-Revision-2.pdf].
- The FCC minimum-perigee rule, 47 CFR § 25.283, applies only to satellites the FCC authorizes, launched on or after 18 March 2002 [47 CFR § 25.283: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/47/25.283]. The register does not say whether the FCC authorized this satellite.
# Cause
Cause category: failed in orbit or broke up before disposal
The operator's release reports an anomaly in orbit after which the satellite could not return to service.
- "The incident resulted in a complete outage of service and is still under investigation in partnership with our satellite provider Boeing Satellite Systems" [MEASAT release: https://www.measat.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/EXT-MEASAT-to-Retire-MEASAT-3-210806.pdf]
- "Despite maintaining continuous telemetry and command control of MEASAT-3, further testing and recovery efforts found that the satellite could not re-enter service." [MEASAT release: https://www.measat.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/EXT-MEASAT-to-Retire-MEASAT-3-210806.pdf]
- The same words appear on a second page that reprints the release [Satellite Evolution: https://www.satelliteevolution.com/post/measat-to-retire-measat-3].
# What this page cannot say
- The register does not say when the satellite stopped working or was moved.
- The register sees only objects still near GEO. Satellites sent much higher, or that left GEO, may be missing.
- Sexton gives a verdict on an orbit under stated assumptions. It is not a legal finding.
