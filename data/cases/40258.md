GEO is the circular orbit about 35,786 km above the equator, where a satellite stays over one spot [register: config.geoAltitudeKm]. Perigee is the lowest point of an orbit. Eccentricity is how far an orbit is from a perfect circle. 0 is a circle [docs/methodology.md: definition of eccentricity].

The perigee is 309 km above GEO [register: pKm]. The apogee is 347 km above GEO [register: qKm]. The eccentricity is 0.0004471 [register: e]. The IADC eccentricity limit is 0.003 [register: config.iadc.eccentricityMax].

Sexton's verdict is "buried": the perigee is above the IADC disposal threshold for every assumed C_R·A/m, and the eccentricity is within the limit [register: verdict.verdict]. The IADC guidelines are voluntary [IADC-02-01 Revision 2: https://orbitaldebris.jsc.nasa.gov/library/IADC-Space-Debris-Guidelines-Revision-2.pdf]. The upper end of the threshold is 280 km above GEO [register: config.thresholdHighKm]. The perigee is 29 km above it [register: verdict.margins.high]. The verdict is not borderline [register: verdict.borderline]. A verdict is borderline when the perigee is within 25 km of a band edge [register: config.uncertaintyKm].

# Assumptions and data age

The verdict assumes a C_R·A/m between 0.012 and 0.045 m²/kg [register: config.iadc.crAmMin] [register: config.iadc.crAmMax]. These are Sexton's assumptions. They are not measured for this satellite. The verdict also allows a mean-element uncertainty of ±25 km [register: config.uncertaintyKm].

The orbit comes from SATCAT, not from GP mean elements [register: orbitSource.kind]. SATCAT gives whole-kilometre altitudes and no element date, so the data age is "SATCAT, element date not given". The snapshot is 2026-11-22 [register: snapshot.set].

# What the sources say

The IADC guidelines are voluntary: organisations are "encouraged" to use them [IADC-02-01 Revision 2: https://orbitaldebris.jsc.nasa.gov/library/IADC-Space-Debris-Guidelines-Revision-2.pdf].

The FCC minimum-perigee rule, 47 CFR § 25.283, applies only to satellites the FCC authorizes: paragraph (a) covers "a space station authorized to operate in the geostationary satellite orbit under this part" [47 CFR § 25.283: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/47/25.283]. Paragraph (d) excludes space stations launched before March 18, 2002 [47 CFR § 25.283: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/47/25.283]. This object was launched on 2014-09-27 [register: launchDate], which is after that date, so the rule's launch-date test is met. Sexton cannot tell whether the FCC authorized this satellite. Where it did not, the rule does not apply.

None of the opened sources in the methodology is about this object. This case file rests on the register alone.
