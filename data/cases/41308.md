GEO is the circular orbit about 35,786 km above the equator, where a satellite stays over one spot. Perigee is the lowest point of an orbit. Eccentricity is how far an orbit is from a perfect circle. 0 is a circle. [docs/methodology.md: definition of eccentricity]

Unquiet means the orbit reaches the protected zone around GEO. The register does not say when the satellite stopped working or was moved.

# What the sources say

- The IADC guidelines say organisations are "encouraged" to use them. [IADC Space Debris Mitigation Guidelines: https://orbitaldebris.jsc.nasa.gov/library/IADC-Space-Debris-Guidelines-Revision-2.pdf]
- The IADC threshold band runs from 247 km to 280 km above GEO under the stated assumptions. [register: config.thresholdLowKm] [register: config.thresholdHighKm]
- The perigee above GEO is -114.0 km, so it is below the IADC disposal threshold under the stated assumptions. [register: pKm]

# Cause

Cause category: failed in orbit or broke up before disposal

The sources report a failure in orbit that the operator could not recover from.

- The quotes below are from an Intelsat statement, as the page prints it [Spaceflight Now: https://spaceflightnow.com/2019/04/22/intelsat-declares-drifting-satellite-total-loss/].
- "the Intelsat 29e propulsion system experienced damage that caused a leak of the propellant on board the satellite resulting in a service disruption to customers on the satellite." [Spaceflight Now: https://spaceflightnow.com/2019/04/22/intelsat-declares-drifting-satellite-total-loss/]
- "While working to recover the satellite, a second anomaly occurred, after which all efforts to recover the satellite were unsuccessful." [Spaceflight Now: https://spaceflightnow.com/2019/04/22/intelsat-declares-drifting-satellite-total-loss/]
