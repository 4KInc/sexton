# What the register shows

GEO is the circular orbit about 35,786 km above the equator, where a satellite stays over one spot. Perigee is the lowest point of an orbit. Eccentricity is how far an orbit is from a perfect circle. 0 is a circle. [docs/methodology.md: definition of eccentricity]

- Name and NORAD id: INTELSAT 33E (IS-33E), 41748. [register: norad]
- Launch date: 2016-08-24. [register: launchDate]
- The catalogue's current status is "retired payload". The source of that status is SATCAT. [register: status] [register: statusSource]
- The register does not say when the satellite stopped working or was moved.
- Perigee above GEO: -100.7 km. [register: pKm]
- Apogee above GEO: +112.9 km. [register: qKm]
- Eccentricity: 0.002533. [register: e]
- Years since launch: 10. This is not an age at the end of operation. [register: verdict.yearsSinceLaunch]
- Verdict: unquiet, not borderline. In the register, unquiet means the orbit reaches the protected zone. The zone extends 200 km either side of GEO. [register: verdict.verdict] [register: verdict.borderline] [register: config.protectedZoneKm]

# Assumptions and data age

- The IADC disposal threshold uses C_R·A/m, which is not known for this satellite. The register assumes a range from 0.012 m²/kg to 0.045 m²/kg. [register: config.iadc.crAmMin] [register: config.iadc.crAmMax]
- The register assumes a mean-element uncertainty of ±25 km. [register: config.uncertaintyKm]
- Data age: the orbit comes from GP mean elements with epoch 2026-11-22T09:58:38.468064. [register: orbitSource.epoch]
- The snapshot set is 2026-11-22. [register: snapshot.set]
- The IADC guidelines are voluntary. The FCC minimum-perigee rule (47 CFR § 25.283) applies only to satellites the FCC authorizes, launched on or after 18 March 2002. [47 CFR § 25.283: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/47/25.283]
- The register does not know whether the FCC authorized this satellite.

# Cause

Cause category: failed in orbit or broke up before disposal

The sources report that the satellite lost power and broke up in orbit.

- "The satellite experienced an anomaly" [DatacenterDynamics: https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/intelsats-33e-geo-satellite-breaks-up-in-orbit/]
- "resulting in a loss of power and service to customers" [DatacenterDynamics: https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/intelsats-33e-geo-satellite-breaks-up-in-orbit/]
- The page prints these two fragments as a statement by Intelsat. [DatacenterDynamics: https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/intelsats-33e-geo-satellite-breaks-up-in-orbit/]
- "U.S. Space Forces-Space (S4S)" [Via Satellite: https://www.satellitetoday.com/connectivity/2024/10/21/intelsats-is-33e-satellite-is-a-total-loss/]

- The page says Intelsat started a failure review board, so it gives no cause of the breakup. [Via Satellite: https://www.satellitetoday.com/connectivity/2024/10/21/intelsats-is-33e-satellite-is-a-total-loss/]
