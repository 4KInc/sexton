GEO is the circular orbit about 35,786 km above the equator, where a satellite stays over one spot [register: config.geoAltitudeKm]. Perigee is the lowest point of an orbit. Eccentricity is how far an orbit is from a perfect circle. 0 is a circle [docs/methodology.md: definition of eccentricity].

The perigee is 17.4 km above GEO [register: pKm]. The apogee is 81.8 km above GEO [register: qKm]. The eccentricity is 0.000763 [register: e]. The IADC eccentricity limit is 0.003 [register: config.iadc.eccentricityMax].

Sexton's verdict is "unquiet": the orbit reaches the protected zone [register: verdict.verdict]. The zone is GEO ± 200 km [register: config.protectedZoneKm]. The perigee is 182.6 km inside the zone's upper edge [register: verdict.margins.zone]. The verdict is not borderline [register: verdict.borderline].

The IADC guidelines are voluntary [IADC-02-01 Revision 2: https://orbitaldebris.jsc.nasa.gov/library/IADC-Space-Debris-Guidelines-Revision-2.pdf]. The perigee is 229.6 km below the low end [register: verdict.margins.low].

# What the sources say

The IADC guidelines are voluntary: organisations are "encouraged" to use them [IADC-02-01 Revision 2: https://orbitaldebris.jsc.nasa.gov/library/IADC-Space-Debris-Guidelines-Revision-2.pdf].

The FCC minimum-perigee rule, 47 CFR § 25.283, applies only to satellites the FCC authorizes: paragraph (a) covers "a space station authorized to operate in the geostationary satellite orbit under this part" [47 CFR § 25.283: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/47/25.283]. Paragraph (d) excludes space stations launched before March 18, 2002 [47 CFR § 25.283: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/47/25.283]. Intelsat 511 was launched on 1985-06-30 [register: launchDate], so the rule's launch-date test is not met. The register is marked "before FCC rule" for this reason [register: verdict.era]. Sexton cannot tell whether the FCC authorized this satellite, and licence conditions can still apply.

None of the opened sources in the methodology is about this satellite. This case file rests on the register alone.

# What this page cannot say

The register does not say when this satellite stopped working or was moved. SATCAT gives a launch date, and GP gives the current orbit. Neither gives any date for the end of its operation.

The register sees only objects still near GEO. Satellites sent much higher, or that left GEO, may be missing.
