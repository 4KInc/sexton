# What the register shows

ANGOSAT 1 has NORAD id 43087 [register: norad]. GEO is the circular orbit about 35,786 km above the equator, where a satellite stays over one spot [register: config.geoAltitudeKm]. Perigee is the lowest point of an orbit. Eccentricity is how far an orbit is from a perfect circle. 0 is a circle [docs/methodology.md: definition of eccentricity].

- The catalogue status is "retired payload", from SATCAT [register: statusSource].
- The launch date is 2017-12-26 [register: launchDate].
- The register counts 8 years from launch to the snapshot [register: verdict.yearsSinceLaunch].
- The perigee is 162 km above GEO [register: pKm].
- The apogee is 345 km above GEO [register: qKm].
- The eccentricity is 0.002157 [register: e]. The IADC limit is 0.003 [register: config.iadc.eccentricityMax].
- The verdict is unquiet, and it is not borderline [register: verdict.verdict] [register: verdict.borderline]. Unquiet means the perigee is at or below the edge of the protected zone, which is 200 km above GEO [register: config.protectedZoneKm].
- The IADC threshold band runs from 247 km to 280 km above GEO [register: config.thresholdLowKm] [register: config.thresholdHighKm]. The perigee is below the bottom of that band, so it is below the IADC disposal threshold under the stated assumptions.

# Assumptions and data age

- The verdict assumes a C_R·A/m from 0.012 m²/kg [register: config.iadc.crAmMin] to 0.045 m²/kg [register: config.iadc.crAmMax].
- The verdict allows ±25 km of mean-element uncertainty [register: config.uncertaintyKm].
- The orbit comes from SATCAT whole-kilometre altitudes. The perigee altitude is 35,948 km and the apogee altitude is 36,131 km [register: perigeeKm] [register: apogeeKm].
- Data age: SATCAT, element date not given [register: orbitSource.kind]. The snapshot set is 2026-11-22 [register: snapshot.set].

# What the sources say

- The IADC guidelines are voluntary. Organisations are "encouraged" to use them [IADC Space Debris Mitigation Guidelines, IADC-02-01 Revision 2: https://orbitaldebris.jsc.nasa.gov/library/IADC-Space-Debris-Guidelines-Revision-2.pdf].
- The FCC minimum-perigee rule (47 CFR § 25.283) applies only to satellites the FCC authorizes, launched on or after 18 March 2002 [47 CFR § 25.283: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/47/25.283].
- The launch date is on or after that date [register: config.fccRuleDate]. The register does not say whether the FCC authorized this satellite.

# Cause

Cause category: failed in orbit or broke up before disposal

The sources report that the satellite stopped working in orbit.

- "Eventually, the satellite stopped sending data and Energia confirmed it was inoperable." [SpaceNews: https://spacenews.com/angola-eyes-new-satellite-as-african-space-race-accelerates/]

# What this page cannot say

- The register does not say when the satellite stopped working or was moved.
- The register only sees objects still near GEO. Satellites sent much higher, or that left GEO, may be missing.
- The register holds no source for the satellite's mission, its operator's plans, or why its orbit is what it is.
