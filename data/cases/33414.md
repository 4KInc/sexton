# What the register shows

GEO is the circular orbit above the equator where a satellite stays over one spot. Perigee is the lowest point of an orbit. Eccentricity is how far an orbit is from a perfect circle. 0 is a circle [docs/methodology.md: definition of eccentricity].

- The register lists VENESAT-1 under NORAD id 33414 [register: norad].
- The catalogue status is "retired payload", taken from SATCAT [register: status] [register: statusSource].
- The launch date is 2008-10-29 [register: launchDate].
- The register counts 18 years since launch [register: verdict.yearsSinceLaunch]. This is a count from launch. It is not an age at the end of operation.
- The perigee is 1 km below GEO, so the register holds pKm as -1 km [register: pKm].
- The apogee is 568 km above GEO [register: qKm].
- The eccentricity is 0.006702 [register: e].
- The verdict is unquiet, and it is not borderline [register: verdict.verdict] [register: verdict.borderline].

Unquiet means the orbit reaches the protected zone around GEO. The register's orbit for this object comes from SATCAT whole-kilometre altitudes, not from GP mean elements [register: orbitSource.kind]. The perigee and apogee above are whole-kilometre values from SATCAT [register: perigeeKm] [register: apogeeKm].

# Assumptions and data age

- The verdict is below the IADC disposal threshold under the stated assumptions. It is a verdict about an orbit, not about an owner.
- C_R·A/m is assumed to lie between 0.012 m²/kg and 0.045 m²/kg [register: config.iadc.crAmMin] [register: config.iadc.crAmMax].
- The mean-element uncertainty is assumed to be ±25 km [register: config.uncertaintyKm].
- The threshold band runs from 247 km to 280 km above GEO [register: config.thresholdLowKm] [register: config.thresholdHighKm].
- Data age: SATCAT, element date not given [register: orbitSource.kind].
- Snapshot set: 2026-11-22 [register: snapshot.set].

# What the sources say

- The IADC guidelines are voluntary. They say organisations are "encouraged" to use them [IADC-02-01 Revision 2: https://orbitaldebris.jsc.nasa.gov/library/IADC-Space-Debris-Guidelines-Revision-2.pdf].
- The FCC minimum-perigee rule (47 CFR § 25.283) applies only to satellites the FCC authorizes, launched on or after 18 March 2002 [47 CFR § 25.283: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/47/25.283]. This satellite's launch date is after that date [register: launchDate]. The register does not say whether the FCC authorized it.

# Cause

Cause category: failed in orbit or broke up before disposal

The source reports that the satellite stopped working in orbit.

- "Venezuela’s only telecommunications satellite has veered off its orbit and stopped working, creating a logistical headache for the cash-strapped nation." [SatNews/AP: https://satnews.com/2020/03/31/venezuelas-only-telecoms-satellite-is-lost-in-space/]
- The source says the satellite was tumbling. It does not say that contact was lost [SatNews/AP: https://satnews.com/2020/03/31/venezuelas-only-telecoms-satellite-is-lost-in-space/].

# What this page cannot say

- The register does not say when the satellite stopped working or was moved.
- The register does not say why the orbit is what it is, beyond the source quoted above.
- The register only sees objects still near GEO. Satellites sent much higher, or that left GEO, may be missing.
