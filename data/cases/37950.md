# What the register shows

GEO is the circular orbit above the equator where a satellite stays over one spot. Perigee is the lowest point of an orbit. Eccentricity is how far an orbit is from a perfect circle. 0 is a circle [docs/methodology.md: definition of eccentricity].

- Catalogue status: retired payload, from SATCAT [register: status] [register: statusSource].
- Owner code in the catalogue: ISRA [register: owner].
- Launch date: 2011-12-11 [register: launchDate].
- Years from launch to the snapshot: 14 [register: verdict.yearsSinceLaunch].

The perigee is 7.7 km above GEO [register: pKm]. The apogee, the highest point, is 22.8 km above GEO [register: qKm]. The eccentricity is 0.0001783 [register: e].

The verdict is unquiet: the orbit reaches the protected zone around GEO. The verdict is not borderline [register: verdict.verdict] [register: verdict.borderline].

The register does not say when the satellite stopped working or was moved.

The verdict is "under the stated assumptions". The assumed C_R·A/m runs from 0.012 m²/kg [register: config.iadc.crAmMin] to 0.045 m²/kg [register: config.iadc.crAmMax]. The mean-element uncertainty is ±25 km [register: config.uncertaintyKm].

Data age: the orbit comes from GP mean elements with epoch 2026-11-22T09:52:07.813920 [register: orbitSource.epoch]. The snapshot set is 2026-11-22 [register: snapshot.set].

The IADC guidelines are voluntary. The FCC minimum-perigee rule (47 CFR § 25.283) applies only to satellites the FCC authorizes, launched on or after 18 March 2002 [47 CFR § 25.283: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/47/25.283]. The register cannot tell whether the FCC authorized this satellite.
