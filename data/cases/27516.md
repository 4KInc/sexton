# What the register shows
GEO is the circular orbit about 35,786 km above the equator, where a satellite stays over one spot [register: config.geoAltitudeKm]. Perigee is the lowest point of an orbit. Eccentricity is how far an orbit is from a perfect circle. 0 is a circle [docs/methodology.md: definition of eccentricity].
- Name and NORAD id: KODAMA (DRTS), 27516 [register: norad].
- Launch date: 2002-09-10 [register: launchDate].
- The catalogue lists it as a retired payload. Status source: SATCAT [register: status] [register: statusSource].
- Years since launch at the snapshot date: 24. This is a count since launch, not an age at the end of operation [register: verdict.yearsSinceLaunch].
- Perigee above GEO: +193 km [register: pKm].
- Apogee above GEO: +374 km [register: qKm].
- Eccentricity: 0.002132 [register: e].
- Verdict: unquiet, and borderline [register: verdict.verdict] [register: verdict.borderline]. Unquiet means the orbit reaches the protected zone, which extends 200 km either side of GEO [register: config.protectedZoneKm].
- The perigee is 7 km under the edge of the zone, which is inside the ±25 km uncertainty [register: verdict.margins.zone] [register: config.uncertaintyKm].
# Assumptions and data age
- The verdict assumes a C_R·A/m between 0.012 and 0.045 m²/kg [register: config.iadc.crAmMin] [register: config.iadc.crAmMax]. These are Sexton's assumptions, not measured for this satellite.
- The verdict allows a mean-element uncertainty of ±25 km [register: config.uncertaintyKm].
- Data age: SATCAT, element date not given [register: orbitSource.kind]. The orbit rests on SATCAT whole-kilometre altitudes, not on GP mean elements.
- Snapshot set: 2026-11-22 [register: snapshot.set].
# What the sources say
- The IADC guidelines are voluntary: organisations are "encouraged" to use them [IADC-02-01 Revision 2: https://orbitaldebris.jsc.nasa.gov/library/IADC-Space-Debris-Guidelines-Revision-2.pdf].
- The FCC minimum-perigee rule, 47 CFR § 25.283, applies only to satellites the FCC authorizes, launched on or after 18 March 2002 [47 CFR § 25.283: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/47/25.283]. The register does not say whether the FCC authorized this satellite.
# Cause
Cause category: disposal reported by operator
The operator's slides report that a disposal was carried out.
- "静止高度より近地点高度を約292㎞上昇させ、離心率を0.003以下に調整。" [JAXA slides for MEXT: https://www.mext.go.jp/b_menu/shingi/gijyutu/gijyutu2/059/shiryo/__icsFiles/afieldfile/2017/10/27/1397652_4.pdf]
- "26.258㎏の推薬排出を確認。" [JAXA slides for MEXT: https://www.mext.go.jp/b_menu/shingi/gijyutu/gijyutu2/059/shiryo/__icsFiles/afieldfile/2017/10/27/1397652_4.pdf]
- The slides are in Japanese. The English reading is ours, not the slides': the perigee altitude was raised about 292 km above the geostationary altitude and the eccentricity was adjusted to 0.003 or less; the venting of 26.258 kg of remaining propellant was confirmed [JAXA slides for MEXT: https://www.mext.go.jp/b_menu/shingi/gijyutu/gijyutu2/059/shiryo/__icsFiles/afieldfile/2017/10/27/1397652_4.pdf].
- Side by side. The operator reports a perigee raised about 292 km [JAXA slides for MEXT: https://www.mext.go.jp/b_menu/shingi/gijyutu/gijyutu2/059/shiryo/__icsFiles/afieldfile/2017/10/27/1397652_4.pdf]. The register reads p = +193 km [register: pKm] and q = +374 km [register: qKm] from SATCAT whole-kilometre altitudes [register: orbitSource.kind]. The GP-PLUS mean elements, kept as evidence only, give +192.9 km and +373.8 km [docs/methodology.md: the story stones that rest on SATCAT altitudes alone]. The operator's figure and the register's differ by more than the ±25 km uncertainty [register: config.uncertaintyKm].
- No source in the Sources table says why the two differ, and this page gives no reason.
# What this page cannot say
- The register does not say when the satellite stopped working or was moved.
- The register sees only objects still near GEO. Satellites sent much higher, or that left GEO, may be missing.
- Sexton gives a verdict on an orbit under stated assumptions. It is not a legal finding.
