# What the register shows

GEO is the circular orbit about 35,786 km above the equator, where a satellite stays over one spot [register: config.geoAltitudeKm]. Perigee is the lowest point of an orbit. Eccentricity is how far an orbit is from a perfect circle. 0 is a circle [docs/methodology.md: definition of eccentricity].

- Name and id: ZHONGXING-9A, NORAD 42763 [register: norad].
- Status in the catalogue: retired payload, from SATCAT [register: statusSource]. The register does not say when the satellite stopped working or was moved.
- Owner code: PRC [register: owner].
- Launch date: 2017-06-18 [register: launchDate]. That is 9 years before the snapshot date [register: verdict.yearsSinceLaunch].
- Perigee above GEO: 122.2 km [register: pKm].
- Apogee above GEO: 150.9 km [register: qKm].
- Eccentricity: 0.0003401 [register: e].
- Verdict: unquiet, not borderline [register: verdict.verdict] [register: verdict.borderline]. Unquiet means the orbit reaches the protected zone.
- The protected zone reaches 200 km either side of GEO [register: config.protectedZoneKm]. The perigee of 122.2 km above GEO [register: pKm] is inside it.

# Assumptions and data age

The IADC threshold is 235 km plus 1000 times C_R·A/m above GEO [register: config.iadc.baseKm] [register: config.iadc.factorKm]. The register does not know C_R·A/m for this satellite. It assumes a range from 0.012 m²/kg to 0.045 m²/kg [register: config.iadc.crAmMin] [register: config.iadc.crAmMax]. That gives a threshold band from 247 km to 280 km above GEO [register: config.thresholdLowKm] [register: config.thresholdHighKm].

Mean elements are assumed good to ±25 km [register: config.uncertaintyKm].

Data age: the orbit comes from GP mean elements with epoch 2026-11-22T09:58:38.468064 [register: orbitSource.epoch]. The snapshot set is 2026-11-22 [register: snapshot.set].

# What the sources say

- The IADC guidelines are voluntary. The guidelines say organisations are "encouraged" to use them [IADC Space Debris Mitigation Guidelines, IADC-02-01 Revision 2: https://orbitaldebris.jsc.nasa.gov/library/IADC-Space-Debris-Guidelines-Revision-2.pdf].
- The FCC minimum-perigee rule, 47 CFR § 25.283, applies only to satellites the FCC authorizes, launched on or after 18 March 2002 [47 CFR § 25.283: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/47/25.283]. The register does not say whether the FCC authorized this satellite.
- The IADC eccentricity limit is 0.003 [IADC Space Debris Mitigation Guidelines, IADC-02-01 Revision 2: https://orbitaldebris.jsc.nasa.gov/library/IADC-Space-Debris-Guidelines-Revision-2.pdf]. The eccentricity of this orbit is 0.0003401 [register: e].
