# What the register shows

GEO is the circular orbit above the equator where a satellite stays over one spot. Perigee is the lowest point of an orbit. Eccentricity is how far an orbit is from a perfect circle. 0 is a circle [docs/methodology.md: definition of eccentricity].

- Catalogue status: retired payload, from SATCAT [register: status] [register: statusSource].
- Launch date: 2010-06-26 [register: launchDate].
- NORAD id: 36744 [register: norad].
- Perigee above GEO: +276 km [register: pKm].
- Apogee above GEO: +348 km [register: qKm].
- Eccentricity: 0.0008475 [register: e].
- Verdict: uncertain, and borderline [register: verdict.verdict] [register: verdict.borderline].

The perigee lies inside the IADC threshold band, from 247 km [register: config.thresholdLowKm] to 280 km above GEO [register: config.thresholdHighKm]. Under the stated assumptions the result depends on the satellite's true C_R·A/m, which the register does not know.

# Assumptions and data age

- C_R·A/m is assumed to lie between 0.012 m²/kg [register: config.iadc.crAmMin] and 0.045 m²/kg [register: config.iadc.crAmMax].
- Mean-element uncertainty is ±25 km [register: config.uncertaintyKm].
- Data age: SATCAT, element date not given [register: orbitSource.kind]. Snapshot set 2026-11-22 [register: snapshot.set].
- The orbit rests on SATCAT whole-kilometre altitudes, not on GP mean elements [register: orbitSource.kind].

The IADC guidelines are voluntary. The FCC minimum-perigee rule (47 CFR § 25.283) applies only to satellites the FCC authorizes, launched on or after 18 March 2002 [47 CFR § 25.283: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/47/25.283]. The register does not say whether the FCC authorized this satellite.

# Cause

Cause category: disposal reported by operator

The sources report that the operator moved the satellite to a disposal orbit.

- "The disposal was carried out by shutting off power to all of the satellite's payloads to end its mission, then moving the satellite to a disposal orbit, or graveyard orbit, about 300 kilometers higher than its existing geostationary orbit (at an altitude of approximately 35,786 kilometers)." [Seoul Economic Daily: https://en.sedaily.com/finance/2026/06/08/koreas-first-geostationary-satellite-chollian-1-ends-16]
- The page reports a statement by the Korea Aerospace Research Institute. It says it is AI-translated from Korean, so the English is a machine translation [Seoul Economic Daily: https://en.sedaily.com/finance/2026/06/08/koreas-first-geostationary-satellite-chollian-1-ends-16].
- Case note's reading: the operator reports a disposal about 300 km above GEO [Seoul Economic Daily: https://en.sedaily.com/finance/2026/06/08/koreas-first-geostationary-satellite-chollian-1-ends-16]. The register reads the perigee from SATCAT whole-kilometre altitudes as +276 km [register: pKm]. The verdict is "uncertain" and borderline [register: verdict.verdict] [register: verdict.borderline]. That is within the ±25 km mean-element uncertainty of the operator's figure [register: config.uncertaintyKm].

# What this page cannot say

- The register does not say when the satellite stopped working or was moved.
- No KARI page is in the Sources table. The only source for the disposal is the news page above.
- The register only sees objects still near GEO. Satellites sent much higher, or that left GEO, may be missing.
