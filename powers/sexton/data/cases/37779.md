# What the register shows

PAKSAT-1R has NORAD id 37779 [register: norad]. The register lists it under the international designator 2011-042A [register: intlDes].

Three terms first. GEO is the circular orbit about 35,786 km above the equator, where a satellite stays over one spot [register: config.geoAltitudeKm]. Perigee is the lowest point of an orbit. Eccentricity is how far an orbit is from a perfect circle, and 0 is a circle [docs/methodology.md: definition of eccentricity].

- Catalogue status: retired payload, from SATCAT [register: status] [register: statusSource].
- Launch date: 2011-08-11 [register: launchDate].
- Years since launch at the snapshot date: 15 [register: verdict.yearsSinceLaunch].
- Owner code in the catalogue: PAKI [register: owner].

The orbit comes from SATCAT whole-kilometre altitudes [register: orbitSource.kind].

- Perigee: 36,027 km altitude [register: perigeeKm].
- Apogee: 36,056 km altitude [register: apogeeKm].
- Perigee above GEO: +241 km [register: pKm].
- Apogee above GEO: +270 km [register: qKm].
- Eccentricity: 0.0003418 [register: e].

The verdict is **shallow grave** [register: verdict.verdict]. This means the perigee is above the protected zone but below the IADC disposal threshold for every assumed C_R·A/m. The verdict is borderline [register: verdict.borderline].

The margins, in km above GEO:

- Against the edge of the protected zone (200 km): +41 km [register: verdict.margins.zone] [register: config.protectedZoneKm].
- Against the low end of the threshold band (247 km): −6 km [register: verdict.margins.low] [register: config.thresholdLowKm].
- Against the high end of the threshold band (280 km): −39 km [register: verdict.margins.high] [register: config.thresholdHighKm].

The perigee is 6 km short of the low end of the band [register: verdict.margins.low]. That is less than the ±25 km uncertainty [register: config.uncertaintyKm]. So the result could fall in the "uncertain" band under the stated assumptions.

# Assumptions and data age

- The IADC threshold is 235 km plus 1000 times C_R·A/m [register: config.iadc.baseKm] [register: config.iadc.factorKm].
- Sexton assumes C_R·A/m between 0.012 and 0.045 m²/kg [register: config.iadc.crAmMin] [register: config.iadc.crAmMax]. The real value for this satellite is not known.
- The mean-element uncertainty is ±25 km [register: config.uncertaintyKm].
- The IADC eccentricity limit is 0.003 [register: config.iadc.eccentricityMax]. The eccentricity here is below it [register: e].
- Data age: SATCAT, element date not given [register: orbitSource.kind]. The snapshot set is 2026-11-22 [register: snapshot.set].

The perigee is below the IADC disposal threshold under the stated assumptions. This is a statement about an orbit, not about the owner.

# What the sources say

- The IADC guidelines are voluntary. They say organisations are "encouraged" to use them [IADC Space Debris Mitigation Guidelines, IADC-02-01 Revision 2: https://orbitaldebris.jsc.nasa.gov/library/IADC-Space-Debris-Guidelines-Revision-2.pdf].
- The FCC minimum-perigee rule, 47 CFR § 25.283, applies only to satellites the FCC authorizes, launched on or after 18 March 2002 [47 CFR § 25.283: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/47/25.283].
- The launch date of this satellite is on or after that date [register: launchDate] [register: config.fccRuleDate]. The register cannot tell whether the FCC authorized it.

# What this page cannot say

- The register does not say when the satellite stopped working or was moved.
- It does not say why the orbit is what it is, or what the operator planned.
- Perigee rests on a whole-kilometre SATCAT altitude, and the orbit may have changed since the data were taken.
- The register only sees objects still near GEO. Satellites sent much higher, or that left GEO, may be missing.
