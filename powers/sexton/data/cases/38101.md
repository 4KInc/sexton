GEO is the circular orbit about 35,786 km above the equator, where a satellite stays over one spot. [register: config.geoAltitudeKm] The protected zone is GEO plus or minus 200 km. [register: config.protectedZoneKm]

Perigee is the lowest point of an orbit. The perigee is 54.3 km below GEO, so the register gives it as -54.3 km above GEO. [register: pKm]

The apogee, the highest point, is 2.7 km above GEO. [register: qKm]

Eccentricity is how far an orbit is from a perfect circle. 0 is a circle. [docs/methodology.md: definition of eccentricity] The eccentricity is 0.0006762. [register: e]

The inclination is 9.211 degrees. [register: inclinationDeg]

# Assumptions and data age

The IADC disposal threshold is 235 km plus 1000 times C_R·A/m, above GEO. [register: config.iadc.baseKm] [register: config.iadc.factorKm] The register does not know C_R·A/m for this satellite. It assumes a range of 0.012 to 0.045 m²/kg. [register: config.iadc.crAmMin] [register: config.iadc.crAmMax] That gives a threshold band from 247 km to 280 km above GEO. [register: config.thresholdLowKm] [register: config.thresholdHighKm]

The register assumes a mean-element uncertainty of ±25 km. [register: config.uncertaintyKm]

The perigee is below the IADC disposal threshold under the stated assumptions, and it is inside the protected zone.

Data age: the orbit comes from GP mean elements with epoch 2026-11-22T12:24:27.219744. [register: orbitSource.epoch] The snapshot set is 2026-11-22. [register: snapshot.set]

# What the sources say

The FCC minimum-perigee rule (47 CFR § 25.283) applies only to satellites the FCC authorizes, launched on or after 18 March 2002. [47 CFR § 25.283: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/47/25.283] The launch date of this satellite is on or after that date. [register: launchDate] [register: config.fccRuleDate] The register cannot tell whether the FCC authorized it.
