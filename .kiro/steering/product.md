# Product

Sexton is a public register of retired geostationary satellites. For each one it shows whether the satellite was "buried" in the graveyard orbit above the geostationary (GEO) belt, as the IADC debris guidelines ask, or is still drifting in the protected zone around the belt.

Entry: Kiroween 2026, Costume Contest category. The graveyard interface is the data model, not decoration.

## Audience

- Space-policy analysts and journalists.
- Regulators' licensing staff.
- Debris researchers.
- University astrodynamics and space-law courses.

## Problem

- The IADC disposal rule is voluntary and only partly followed. The rule: perigee raised at least 235 km + 1000·C_R·A/m above GEO, with eccentricity ≤ 0.003.
- The FCC's first debris fine (DA 23-888, DISH, EchoStar-7, $150,000) found a disposal of about 122 km above GEO instead of the planned 300 km.
- ESA's 2025 space environment report says 70–95% of GEO retirements succeed.
- Roberts (Georgia Tech, 2025) published the audit method academically.
- Sexton is the public, per-satellite, explorable instrument for that method. It credits Roberts wherever the method is described.

## Not in scope

- Predicting collisions.
- Propagating orbits for 100 years.
- Legal judgements. See language.md.
